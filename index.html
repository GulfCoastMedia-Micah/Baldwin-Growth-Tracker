Story leads from the data

    Where the county is growing

    New housing units authorized by building permits, and population, 2015–2025. Commercial construction is tracked through state construction-stormwater permits (see the map and watershed sections).

    Who is coming — and from where

    IRS migration data tracks households whose tax-return address moved into or out of Baldwin County between filing years. It is the most reliable public measure of who is relocating.

    Map: construction, watersheds and impaired waters

    Each dot is a construction site that got state stormwater coverage (required for disturbing 1+ acre). Dark rings mark sites that regulators cited; ▲ marks sites that were fined. Red lines are waters the state lists as impaired. Source: EPA ECHO / ADEM · EPA ATTAINS · USGS watersheds · IRS ZIP data

    Watersheds feeding Mobile Bay & Perdido Bay

    Construction activity, enforcement and water quality rolled up by HUC-12 watershed (USGS). Click a row to zoom the map.

    Accountability: who's been cited or fined

    Enforcement recorded by ADEM/EPA under the Clean Water Act. Informal actions are notices of violation and warning letters; formal actions are administrative orders, usually with penalties. ECHO reports these for roughly the last 5 years. Each row links to the federal record.

    Outlook

    What's in the pipeline now, and where current trends point. Projections simply extend the 2020–2025 growth rate; they are not official forecasts.

    Sources & attribution

    How to attribute

    • Credit the agency that produced the data, not this dashboard. Each source below has a suggested credit line and links to the original files.
    • Numbers this dashboard calculated (growth percentages, net migration totals, per-100 km² rates, 2030 projections, operator totals, estimated start years and the story leads) should be credited as “[Outlet] analysis of [agency] data.”
    • Data downloaded Oct 29, 2026. Agencies revise their figures, so check the live source before publishing a specific number.
    • Baldwin County's code in Census and IRS files is state 01, county 003 (FIPS 01003). Use it to find the county's rows in the raw files.

    Who's moving in (migration)

    Credit: Internal Revenue Service, Statistics of Income, county-to-county migration data

    Used in: “Net new residents” total; Moving in vs. moving out chart; Newcomer vs. resident income chart; Top places new residents came from; Movers by origin state; income and out-of-state story lead.

    • IRS migration data and user guides ↗
    • Raw files for each filing-year pair, from countyinflow1415.csv to countyinflow2223.csv. Outflows: countyoutflow2223.csv (same pattern).

    “People” is the number of individuals on tax returns (IRS column n2). “Households” is the number of returns (n1). Income is adjusted gross income divided by returns, in nominal dollars. The IRS hides flows under 20 households, so origin totals undercount.

    Construction sites, citations & fines

    Credit: Alabama Department of Environmental Management (ADEM) records, via the U.S. EPA Enforcement and Compliance History Online (ECHO) database

    Used in: “Construction sites” and “Sites cited” totals; map dots; New construction-stormwater sites per year chart; site and citation counts in the Watershed scorecard; Where construction is active chart; both Accountability tables; biggest-fine story lead.

    • Every site in the tables and map links to its own EPA Detailed Facility Report, the citable record for that site's permit, inspections, violations and penalties.
    • ECHO facility search ↗: choose Clean Water Act, state Alabama, county Baldwin to rebuild the full list.
    • ECHO bulk data downloads (ICIS-NPDES) ↗
    • ADEM eFile ↗: the state's own copies of permits, notices of violation and consent orders. Search by permit number (for example ALR10…), which is the ID shown on each EPA report.

    Enforcement and penalties cover roughly the last five years. ADEM re-issues the construction general permit (Apr 2016, Apr 2021, 2026), which resets coverage dates. For those sites, start years marked “est.” are this dashboard's estimate from the sequential permit number. ECHO has noticeably fewer sites starting in 2015–2016, likely a records gap. Residential/commercial type comes from the operator's NAICS industry code and is sometimes missing. Operator names were lightly cleaned up (for example, “LLC” and “Inc.” variants merged). Confirm the exact legal name on the EPA or ADEM record before you name a company.
